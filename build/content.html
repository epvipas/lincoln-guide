📜 A short history of Lincoln

Roman Lindum. The Romans built a legionary fortress on the hilltop in the AD 60s, and by about AD 90 it had become Lindum Colonia, a city for retired soldiers. Newport Arch, the Fossdyke canal and the Brayford Pool harbour all date from Roman times, and the street plan uphill still follows the Roman grid.

Vikings and Normans. After the Romans left, Lincoln became one of the Five Boroughs of the Danelaw. In 1068 William the Conqueror built the castle inside the old Roman walls, and the first cathedral was consecrated in 1092 on the hill opposite.

Medieval glory. After an earthquake damaged the cathedral in 1185, Bishop Hugh began the Gothic rebuilding. When its central spire was finished in 1311, Lincoln Cathedral became the tallest building in the world, a title it held until the spire blew down in 1548. Wool and cloth made the city rich: "Lincoln green" was a famous dye. Two battles of Lincoln were fought here, in 1141 and 1217.

Steam and tanks. In the 1800s engineering firms such as Ruston and Clayton & Shuttleworth made Lincoln a centre of steam engines and machinery. In 1915–16 William Foster & Co. built the world's first tanks here. In the Second World War the county's airfields made Lincolnshire "Bomber County".

Today the University of Lincoln, founded in 1996, has revived the Brayford waterfront. The uphill Cathedral Quarter around the Castle and Bailgate is one of the best-preserved historic city centres in England.

⛰️ Uphill and downhill

Lincoln comes in two halves. Downhill has the river, the station, the High Street and the Brayford. Uphill, on the limestone ridge, has the Cathedral, the Castle and the old Roman Bailgate. Joining them is Steep Hill, a cobbled street so steep that it has handrails, lined with some of the city's best independent shops and tearooms.

🥾 The Steep Hill climb

A slow walk of about a mile from the river to the Roman arch. Tap a name to see each stop.

The steepest stretch is between the Jew's House and Browns Pie Shop. Take it at your own pace and stop for tea halfway.

🍞 Lincolnshire on a plate

  • Plum bread: a rich, fruity tea loaf, sliced and spread with butter, often eaten with cheese.
  • Lincolnshire sausage: coarse pork sausage seasoned with sage.
  • Lincolnshire Poacher: a nutty farmhouse cheddar-style cheese made near Alford.
  • Haslet: a sage-scented pork meatloaf, sliced cold in sandwiches.
